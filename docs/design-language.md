# Design Language

Socarengue Studio should feel like a discovered archive of emotional transmissions.

The design language should be atmospheric, mythic, musical, emotionally immersive, cinematic, mysterious, textured, analog, and noir-neon.

## Experience Keywords

- discovered
- transmitted
- archival
- cinematic
- intimate
- nocturnal
- textured
- musical
- mythic
- analog
- luminous
- haunted but usable

## Visual Direction

The interface should combine clarity with mystery.

Use:

- dark cinematic grounds
- controlled neon accents
- warm paper or aged document textures in moderation
- soft signal-like highlights
- restrained borders and dividers
- filmic spacing
- editorial typography
- artifact-like metadata blocks
- image-led story moments

Avoid:

- bright generic SaaS dashboards
- gamified clutter
- heavy glassmorphism
- endless card grids
- corporate gradients
- sterile CMS tables as the dominant mood
- Patreon-style membership pages

## Palette Philosophy

The palette should feel noir-neon, but not one-note.

Potential color families:

- ink black
- oxidized blue
- deep violet in small doses
- signal cyan
- sodium amber
- old paper ivory
- blood red accent
- tarnished silver

Use neon like a signal, not wallpaper.

## Typography

Typography should support two modes:

- **Archive clarity:** readable interface text, metadata, labels, notes.
- **Cinematic presence:** chapter titles, artifact names, release titles, scene headers.

Guidelines:

- Keep body text highly legible.
- Use display type sparingly.
- Let headings feel like labels on found material or title cards.
- Avoid novelty fonts for core reading.
- Avoid making every surface dramatic; contrast creates atmosphere.

## Layout Language

Studio layouts should be calm and operational.

Audience layouts should feel sequenced and cinematic.

Patterns:

- narrow reading measures for prose
- full-bleed or near full-bleed comic moments where appropriate
- side metadata panels for archive objects
- ordered timelines for scenes and releases
- object detail pages for artifacts, tracks, characters, and lore
- low-noise navigation
- deliberate transitions between reading and archive exploration

## Interaction Language

Interactions should feel tactile and quiet.

Use:

- reveal states
- drawers for contextual detail
- timeline scrubbing for sequences
- minimal hover glows
- subtle audio state indicators
- chapter and cue markers
- artifact linking
- keyboard-friendly controls

Avoid:

- loud achievement-style feedback
- excessive animation
- surprise audio autoplay
- interface patterns that hide important creator operations

## Sound-Aware UX

Soundtrack integration should be visible but respectful.

Design rules:

- never require audio
- never autoplay without clear permission
- always show play, pause, and mute states
- make cues readable as part of the story
- let the reader continue silently
- treat track art, notes, and cue labels as archive material

Sound controls should feel like part of the reading surface, not a generic embedded player pasted onto the page.

## Artifact Design

Artifacts are story objects with atmosphere and structure.

Artifact pages may include:

- title
- classification
- image or media
- transcript or description
- linked characters
- linked scenes
- linked tracks
- public note
- hidden creator note in studio mode
- release appearance history

Audience-facing artifacts should feel discovered.

Creator-facing artifacts should remain editable, searchable, and clear.

## Studio Design

The creator operating system should feel elegant, focused, and alive.

Studio surfaces should prioritize:

- what is being made
- what state it is in
- what it connects to
- what needs attention next

The studio should use atmosphere as an undertone, not as an obstacle.

## Public Design

The public experience should not open with a generic marketing landing page.

The first impression should reveal:

- the story world
- the latest or featured transmission
- the archive nature of the platform
- the relationship between image, text, music, and artifact

Public pages should feel like entering the work, not being sold the work.

## Accessibility

Atmosphere must not compromise access.

Requirements:

- readable contrast
- alt text for key images
- transcripts for audio where meaningful
- no required audio-only information
- no flashing or disorienting motion
- keyboard-accessible controls
- clear focus states
- responsive layouts

Accessibility is part of the archive philosophy: transmissions should be receivable.
