# Visual Symbolism

Socarengue should communicate story through symbols, textures, repeated images, color, light, and material traces.

The visual system should feel noir-neon, analog, humid, archival, emotionally charged, and cinematic.

## Symbolic Vocabulary

Potential recurring symbols:

- vinyl grooves
- cassette tape
- waveform lines
- cracked speakers
- handwritten lyrics
- gold chains
- saints and street shrines
- rain on neon
- old photographs
- city maps
- streetlights
- dance floors
- keys
- mirrors
- moth-eaten posters
- broken glass
- candles
- train lines
- palms and fingerprints
- signal static

Symbols should not become decoration. Each recurring symbol should carry story meaning, character association, or resonance history.

## Color Logic

Potential color families:

- deep shadow
- oxidized blue
- storm gray
- sodium amber
- warm gold
- blood red
- signal cyan
- humid green
- old paper ivory
- tarnished silver

Use color as emotional notation:

- gold for inheritance, warmth, sacred charge, or legacy
- cyan for signal, distance, or transmission
- red for rupture, blood memory, danger, or desire
- blue for night, grief, depth, or city rain
- ivory for recovered documents, archives, or fragile truth

Avoid one-note neon. The world should glow from inside the dark.

## Texture Language

Use textures that imply handling, memory, and decay:

- paper grain
- scanned edges
- analog noise
- tape hiss visuals
- worn ink
- photocopy artifacts
- dust
- water damage
- scratched plastic
- stage light haze
- fabric closeups

Texture should support atmosphere without making the interface hard to read.

## Visual Continuity Metadata

Future visual assets should track:

- character association
- location
- issue
- scene
- artifact links
- emotional tone
- resonance family
- color motif
- symbol motif
- continuity notes
- contributor attribution
- prompt/version history when AI-generated

## Public vs Studio Visuals

Public visuals should feel curated and intentional.

Do not expose:

- repetitive generation attempts
- failed drafts
- low-signal process clutter
- raw continuity notes

Studio visuals can include messy process materials, but they should still be organized around story usefulness.

## Design Warning

Do not mistake darkness for depth.

The visual language should have contrast, breath, warmth, and human trace. Mystery comes from emotional charge and selective revelation, not from hiding everything in black.
